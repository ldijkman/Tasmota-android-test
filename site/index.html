A free Android app that finds every Tasmota and Shelly switch, plug and lamp on your Wi-Fi in seconds. Switch them, group them, and give them timers that follow sunrise and sunset.

What is this about?

  1. Smart plugs and switchesSome lamps and plugs have a tiny computer inside, with Wi-Fi. Many run the free program Tasmota, others are Shellys with their own software. This app works with both.OpenBeken devices partly work too.
  2. They talk over your Wi-FiJust like your phone, they are connected to the Wi-Fi in your house.
  3. This app is the remote controlIt finds them by itself. Tap a picture and the lamp goes on. Tap again and it goes off.
  4. The switch remembersTell it "on when it gets dark, off at 11". It keeps doing that, even when your phone is off.
  5. It all stays at homeNo company in between, no account, nothing sent over the internet. No dead devices when a company stops its support or goes bankrupt: your switches keep working.

The timers live in the switch.

Set them once in the app. After that the switch runs them by itself, even when your phone is off or out of the house.

Each switch holds up to 8 on/off schedules. Use a fixed time, or sunrise and sunset with an offset, and pick the days.

An easy intuitive visual preview shows every schedule on a 24-hour timeline: the yellow line is daylight, from sunrise to sunset, and the orange block is when the switch is on. You see right away if it fits. Obsessed: wasted time on time setting 😉

Copy schedules between devices: set them once, then copy them to another switch. Even from a Tasmota to a Shelly, and back.

Everything for your Tasmota and Shelly devices, in one small app.

Under 250 KB, starts in a moment, and talks straight to your devices over your own Wi-Fi.

  • Finds them in seconds

    Scans your network and lists every Tasmota and Shelly device with its name and address. Recognises them by MAC address when their address changes.

  • Timers with sunrise and sunset

    Up to 8 on/off schedules per switch, stored in the switch. See everything that switches today at a glance.

  • Groups and scenes

    Switch a whole room at once, or set a mood like Movie night with one tap: some lights on, others off.

  • Lamps

    Brightness, warm to cool white, and colour for Tasmota lights and LED strips.

  • Tiles and widgets

    Big tiles in the app, and switches, groups and scenes right on your home screen.

  • Add new devices

    Give a brand-new or reset Tasmota device your Wi-Fi from the app. No browser, no 192.168.4.1.

  • Auto-off

    Let a switch turn itself off after a set time, like a bathroom fan or a heater. It runs in the switch, so it also works when you press the button on the device itself. Cards, tiles and widgets count down.

  • Energy use

    Plugs with a power meter show how many watts they use right now, on the card and the tile.

  • Wall panel

    Turn a spare phone or tablet into a control panel: the screen stays on, dims by itself, and fills the whole screen. It doubles as the Tailscale VPN server for control from anywhere.

  • From anywhere

    Reach your switches away from home with Tailscale VPN, without opening ports on your router.

A wall panel from a spare phone or tablet (kiosk mode)

It also makes the ideal Tailscale VPN server: a phone or tablet on the wall stays at home, on the charger and awake, so you can control your switches from anywhere.

Questions people ask.

Something else? Ask it on GitHub.

Which devices work?

Any device that runs Tasmota firmware: Sonoff, Athom and other plugs, relays, wall switches, lamps and LED strips with an ESP8266 or ESP32 chip.

Shelly devices with their own firmware: Gen2 and newer (Plus, Pro, Mini, Gen3, Gen4) with switching, bulbs, schedules and auto-off; Gen1 partly.

Devices with other firmware, such as eWeLink or Tuya, need Tasmota installed first.

Does my lamp go via a server in China?

No, your lamp stays home. When you tap a switch, the signal travels a few metres over your own Wi-Fi, straight from your phone to the switch. Many smart home apps first send it to a cloud server, sometimes on the other side of the world, and then back again, just to switch on the lamp right next to you. With this app, the trip to the lamp is shorter than the trip to the fridge.

Which phones can run it?

Android 7 or newer. Adding a brand-new device from the app needs Android 10 or newer. There is no iPhone version, but you can try the live demo in any browser.

Why is it not in the Play Store?

I make this app alone, in my spare time. Getting a new app into the Play Store means paying a fee, an identity check, and first finding 12 testers who keep it installed for 14 days. That is a lot of hassle for a free hobby app. So you download it straight from GitHub instead.

Android warns about installing it. Is it safe?

The app is not in the Play Store, so Google Play Protect has not seen it before. You can let Play Protect scan it first, or install without scanning. The full source code is public on GitHub, and GitHub itself builds the download from that source. Since 21 November 2026 I am a Google-registered developer, and the app is registered with Google’s Android developer verification. On most phones Android no longer asks to scan it.

Does it work with OpenBeken or Shelly?

OpenBeken devices partly work: switching does, schedules do not. Shelly devices work fully when they run Tasmota firmware.

New and untested: the app can now also find Shelly devices with their own firmware, switch them on and off, set brightness, white and colour on Shelly bulbs, rename them and set their auto-off, and add a new Shelly to your Wi-Fi (Gen1 to Gen4). Tested on a real Shelly 1 Gen3. Schedules work too on Gen2 and newer (same schedule cards, with sunrise and sunset), stored in the Shelly itself. Energy for Shelly is not there yet.

Why not use the Shelly app?

You don’t have to choose. Both apps can be on your phone and control the same Shelly.

The Shelly app is made by Shelly itself. It works with every Shelly, and only with Shelly. Also when you are away from home, and with Alexa or Google Home. You need a Shelly account for it.

This app is smaller and simpler. No account. Everything stays in your home. Your Tasmota and Shelly switches are on one screen.

They work side by side. Switch the lamp on in one app, and the other app shows it on too. Auto-off and schedules are stored in the Shelly itself, so they keep working, whichever app you used.

Note: this app makes schedules as on/off pairs: one on time and one off time per schedule. Schedules you make in the Shelly app may not show up in this app, but they keep working in the Shelly.

A good mix: the Shelly app to set up a new Shelly, this app for every day.

Why does it ask for my location?

For sunrise and sunset times, which the app sends to your switches. Android also needs location permission before an app can see nearby Wi-Fi names. Your location only goes to your own switches.

What is a VPN?

A VPN (virtual private network) is a private, encrypted connection between your own devices over the internet. With Tailscale VPN, your phone away from home connects to an old phone or tablet at home, as if you were on your home Wi-Fi. Only your own devices can use it, and you don't need to open any ports in your router. Tailscale is free for personal use.

Read the Tailscale VPN guide

How do I get updates?

Scroll to the bottom of the main screen and tap Check for update. The app downloads the newest version from GitHub and installs it.

How many switches can I add?

As many as you like. There is no limit on switches, groups, scenes or widgets, and no paid version that unlocks more. Only Tasmota itself has a limit: up to 8 on/off schedules per switch.

Fun fact:
Tasmota and Shelly share the same roots:
both started on the ESP8266,
a cheap Wi-Fi chip from Espressif.
Espressif was founded in Shanghai in 2008
by Teo Swee Ann from Singapore.
The ESP8266 came out in 2014,
and over 1.5 billion of their chips have been sold.

Fun fact:
The ESP8266 started as a 2-dollar Wi-Fi modem,
controlled with AT commands
from the 1980s telephone modem era.
At first, the documentation was hard to find,
and only in Chinese.
Hobbyists found out it could run its own programs,
and Tasmota and Shelly grew from that.

More: from modem to small computer
  • 2014: the ESP-01 board was first used as a cheap Wi-Fi add-on for an Arduino. The Arduino sent it AT commands, just like a modem.
  • Late 2014: Espressif released an official SDK, and NodeMCU appeared, which let you program the ESP8266 itself in the Lua language. This was the first big step from “modem” to “small computer”.
  • 2015: Ivan Grokhotkov made the ESP8266 Arduino core. From then on you could program the ESP8266 directly from the Arduino IDE, without an Arduino board at all. That’s what made it explode in popularity.
  • Tasmota is built on that ESP8266 Arduino core, so it grew out of the Arduino world.
