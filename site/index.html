Your Tasmota and Shelly switches, one tap away.

A free Android app that finds every Tasmota and Shelly switch, plug and lamp on your Wi-Fi in seconds. Switch them, group them, and give them timers that follow sunrise and sunset.

No cloud, no account, no ads, no device limit. Free and open source.
Works with Tasmota and Shelly; OpenBeken partly.

What is this about?

  1. Smart plugs and switchesSome lamps and plugs have a tiny computer inside, with Wi-Fi. Many run the free program Tasmota, others are Shellys with their own software. This app works with both.OpenBeken devices partly work too.
  2. They talk over your Wi-FiJust like your phone, they are connected to the Wi-Fi in your house.
  3. This app is the remote controlIt finds them by itself. Tap a picture and the lamp goes on. Tap again and it goes off.
  4. The switch remembersTell it "on when it gets dark, off at 11". It keeps doing that, even when your phone is off.
  5. It all stays at homeNo company in between, no account, nothing sent over the internet. No dead devices when a company stops its support or goes bankrupt: your switches keep working.

The timers live in the switch.

Set them once in the app. After that the switch runs them by itself, even when your phone is off or out of the house.

Each switch holds up to 8 on/off schedules. Use a fixed time, or sunrise and sunset with an offset, and pick the days.

An easy intuitive visual preview shows every schedule on a 24-hour timeline: the yellow line is daylight, from sunrise to sunset, and the orange block is when the switch is on. You see right away if it fits. Obsessed: wasted time on time setting 😉

Everything for your Tasmota and Shelly devices, in one small app.

Under 250 KB, starts in a moment, and talks straight to your devices over your own Wi-Fi.

  • Finds them in seconds

    Scans your network and lists every Tasmota and Shelly device with its name and address. Recognises them by MAC address when their address changes.

  • Timers with sunrise and sunset

    Up to 8 on/off schedules per switch, stored in the switch. See everything that switches today at a glance.

  • Groups and scenes

    Switch a whole room at once, or set a mood like Movie night with one tap: some lights on, others off.

  • Lamps

    Brightness, warm to cool white, and colour for Tasmota lights and LED strips.

  • Tiles and widgets

    Big tiles in the app, and switches, groups and scenes right on your home screen.

  • Add new devices

    Wi-Fi provisioning for Tasmota, device inclusion for Shelly: both straight from the app. No browser, no Shelly app, no cloud account.

  • Auto-off

    Let a switch turn itself off after a set time, like a bathroom fan or a heater. It runs in the switch, so it also works when you press the button on the device itself. Cards, tiles and widgets count down.

  • Energy use

    Plugs with a power meter show how many watts they use right now, on the card and the tile.

  • Wall panel

    Turn a spare phone or tablet into a control panel: the screen stays on, dims by itself, and fills the whole screen. It doubles as the Tailscale VPN server for control from anywhere.

  • From anywhere

    Reach your switches away from home with Tailscale VPN, without opening ports on your router.

Example: the thermostat as a script in the Shelly
A little bit tested on a Shelly 1 Gen3. A first idea, not in the app.

❄️ No frost guard in the script: a script can stop. For frost protection use mechanical hardware: a bimetal frost thermostat wired in parallel with the Shelly relay, plus the boiler’s own frost protection. 🔥 Against overheating: a normally closed (NC) max thermostat, for example 30 °C, in series with the relay.

💡 Runs inside the Shelly: no browser, app or computer needs to stay open. The Shelly runs the JavaScript itself, also without internet, and starts it again after a power cut.

// Simple thermostat for a Shelly Gen2/Gen3 with the Sensor Add-on (DS18B20)
// EXAMPLE, A LITTLE BIT TESTED: try it with a lamp on the relay before you connect the boiler.
// NO FROST GUARD in this script: a script can stop. For frost protection use MECHANICAL HARDWARE:
// a bimetal frost thermostat wired in parallel with the Shelly relay (like the old Honeywell ones),
// plus the boiler's own frost protection. Those also work without power, Wi-Fi or software.
// For safety against overheating: a normally closed (NC) max thermostat (e.g. 30 °C) in series with the relay.
// Start with TEST_MODE = true and read the log; set it to false to really switch.
//
// Paste on the Shelly's own web page: Scripts -> Create script -> paste -> Save -> Start.
// Turn on "Run on startup" so it starts again after a power cut.
// Watch what it does in the log under the script (lots of console.log lines),
// or open the log page in Chrome to copy it: http://<shelly-ip>/script/<script-number>/log
// (the exact address is in the first log line).

// ================= SETTINGS: change these =================

let TEST_MODE        = true;    // true  = only write in the log, never switch the relay
                                // false = really switch the relay (heating)

let ROOM_TEMPERATURE = 20.0;    // the temperature you want (°C), the app can change it via KVS "th_set"
let SWITCH_MARGIN    = 0.3;     // heat below 19.7, stop above 20.3

let SENSOR           = 100;     // Add-on sensor number (see the Shelly page: Temperature (100))

let CHECK_EVERY_SECONDS = 30;   // how often to look at the temperature
let MIN_MINUTES_BETWEEN = 3;    // protect the boiler: wait between switching
let SAFETY_OFF_MINUTES  = 10;   // relay turns off by itself if the script stops

// ================= do not change below this line =================

let CFG = {
  dryRun: TEST_MODE,
  sensor: SENSOR,
  setpoint: ROOM_TEMPERATURE,
  hyst: SWITCH_MARGIN,
  minSwitchSec: MIN_MINUTES_BETWEEN * 60,
  everySec: CHECK_EVERY_SECONDS,
  watchdogSec: SAFETY_OFF_MINUTES * 60
};

let state = { heating: false, lastSwitch: 0, mode: "on", round: 0 };  // mode: "on" or "off" (KVS "th_mode")

// clock time for the log: "04:43:12" (local time of the Shelly),
// or the seconds since start ("29332s") as long as the Shelly does not know the time yet
function clock() {
  let sys = Shelly.getComponentStatus("sys");
  if (sys && sys.time && sys.unixtime) {
    let sec = sys.unixtime % 60;
    return sys.time + ":" + (sec < 10 ? "0" : "") + sec;
  }
  return (sys ? sys.uptime : 0) + "s";
}

// keep the last log lines in memory for the log page (small: the Shelly has little memory)
let LOG = [];
let LOG_MAX = 60;
// one decimal, rounded the same way everywhere (26.55 -> "26.6")
function f1(x) {
  return (Math.round(x * 10) / 10).toFixed(1);
}

function log(msg) {
  console.log("[thermostat] " + msg);
  LOG.push(clock() + " " + msg);
  if (LOG.length > LOG_MAX) LOG.splice(0, LOG.length - LOG_MAX);
}

// log page: http://<shelly-ip>/script/<script-number>/log  (plain text, easy to copy)
HTTPServer.registerEndpoint("log", function (req, res) {
  res.code = 200;
  res.headers = [["Content-Type", "text/plain; charset=utf-8"]];
  res.body = "Thermostat log (last " + LOG.length + " lines, newest at the bottom)\n\n" + LOG.join("\n") + "\n";
  res.send();
});

// read the settings the app could write in KVS (the Shelly's own storage)
function loadSettings() {
  log("reading settings from KVS ...");
  Shelly.call("KVS.Get", { key: "th_set" }, function (r, err) {
    if (err) { log("KVS th_set not found (error " + err + "), keep setpoint " + CFG.setpoint); return; }
    if (r && r.value !== undefined) {
      let v = Number(r.value);
      if (v >= 5 && v <= 30) {
        if (v !== CFG.setpoint) log("setpoint changed: " + CFG.setpoint + " -> " + v + " °C");
        CFG.setpoint = v;
      } else {
        log("KVS th_set = " + r.value + " is out of range (5-30), ignored");
      }
    }
  });
  Shelly.call("KVS.Get", { key: "th_mode" }, function (r, err) {
    if (err) { log("KVS th_mode not found, keep mode " + state.mode); return; }
    if (r && (r.value === "on" || r.value === "off")) {
      if (r.value !== state.mode) log("mode changed: " + state.mode + " -> " + r.value);
      state.mode = r.value;
    } else {
      log("KVS th_mode = " + (r ? r.value : "?") + " is not on/off, ignored");
    }
  });
}

// room temperature from the sensor, or null if it gives no value
function roomTemp() {
  let st = Shelly.getComponentStatus("temperature", CFG.sensor);
  if (st && typeof st.tC === "number") {
    log("sensor " + CFG.sensor + ": " + f1(st.tC) + " °C");
    return st.tC;
  }
  log("sensor " + CFG.sensor + ": NO VALUE (not connected, wrong number or broken?)");
  return null;
}

function setHeating(on, why) {
  if (on === state.heating) {
    log("heating stays " + (on ? "ON" : "OFF") + " (" + why + ")");
    return;
  }
  let now = Shelly.getComponentStatus("sys").uptime;
  let since = now - state.lastSwitch;
  // switching off for safety is always allowed; otherwise wait the minimum time
  if (why !== "safety" && state.lastSwitch > 0 && since < CFG.minSwitchSec) {
    log("want heating " + (on ? "ON" : "OFF") + " but last switch was " + since + " s ago, wait until " + CFG.minSwitchSec + " s");
    return;
  }
  if (CFG.dryRun) {
    log("TEST MODE: would turn heating " + (on ? "ON" : "OFF") + " (" + why + "), relay NOT switched");
  } else {
    log("switching heating " + (on ? "ON" : "OFF") + " (" + why + ")" + (on ? ", watchdog " + CFG.watchdogSec + " s" : ""));
    let args = on ? { id: 0, on: true, toggle_after: CFG.watchdogSec } : { id: 0, on: false };
    Shelly.call("Switch.Set", args, function (r, err, msg) {
      if (err) log("ERROR switching relay: " + err + " " + msg);
      else log("relay switched " + (on ? "ON" : "OFF") + ", was " + (r && r.was_on ? "ON" : "OFF"));
    });
  }
  state.heating = on;
  state.lastSwitch = now;
}

// watchdog: while heating, set "on, and off again after watchdogSec" every check.
// Each refresh starts the timer over; if the script stops, the Shelly switches off by itself.
function refreshWatchdog() {
  if (!state.heating) return;
  if (CFG.dryRun) { log("TEST MODE: would refresh watchdog (" + CFG.watchdogSec + " s)"); return; }
  Shelly.call("Switch.Set", { id: 0, on: true, toggle_after: CFG.watchdogSec }, function (r, err, msg) {
    if (err) log("ERROR refreshing watchdog: " + err + " " + msg);
    else log("watchdog refreshed: relay turns off by itself in " + CFG.watchdogSec + " s if the script stops");
  });
}

function check() {
  state.round++;
  // compare with the real relay: it may have been switched by hand, by the watchdog or a schedule
  if (!CFG.dryRun) {
    let sw = Shelly.getComponentStatus("switch", 0);
    let real = !!(sw && sw.output);
    if (real !== state.heating) {
      log("relay is " + (real ? "ON" : "OFF") + " but script thought " + (state.heating ? "ON" : "OFF") + " (switched by hand, watchdog or schedule?)");
      state.heating = real;
    }
  }
  log("---- check " + state.round + " · setpoint " + CFG.setpoint + " °C · mode " + state.mode +
      " · heating " + (state.heating ? "ON" : "OFF") + (CFG.dryRun ? " · TEST MODE" : "") + " ----");
  let t = roomTemp();
  if (t === null) {                       // no sensor works: heating off
    log("no temperature: heating off for safety");
    setHeating(false, "safety");
    return;
  }
  if (state.mode === "off") {
    setHeating(false, "mode off");
    return;
  }
  let low = CFG.setpoint - CFG.hyst, high = CFG.setpoint + CFG.hyst;
  log("heat below " + f1(low) + " °C, stop above " + f1(high) + " °C");
  if (t < low) setHeating(true, f1(t) + " < " + f1(low));
  else if (t > high) setHeating(false, f1(t) + " > " + f1(high));
  else log(f1(t) + " °C is between " + f1(low) + " and " + f1(high) + ": no change (heating stays " + (state.heating ? "ON" : "OFF") + ")");
  refreshWatchdog();
}

// start: read the relay as it is now, then check every 30 s (settings every 5 min)
let wifi = Shelly.getComponentStatus("wifi");
log("log page: http://" + (wifi && wifi.sta_ip ? wifi.sta_ip : "<shelly-ip>") + "/script/" + Shelly.getCurrentScriptId() + "/log");
let sw = Shelly.getComponentStatus("switch", 0);
state.heating = !!(sw && sw.output);
log("script started · TEST MODE " + (TEST_MODE ? "on (relay is never switched)" : "OFF (relay really switches!)") +
    " · room " + f1(ROOM_TEMPERATURE) + " °C · margin " + SWITCH_MARGIN + " · no frost guard (use a mechanical one)");
log("sensor " + SENSOR + " · check every " + CHECK_EVERY_SECONDS + " s · wait " + MIN_MINUTES_BETWEEN +
    " min between switching · safety off after " + SAFETY_OFF_MINUTES + " min · relay is now " + (state.heating ? "ON" : "OFF"));
loadSettings();
Timer.set(CFG.everySec * 1000, true, check);
Timer.set(300 * 1000, true, loadSettings);
check();

Questions people ask.

Something else? Ask it on GitHub.

Which devices work?

Any device that runs Tasmota firmware: Sonoff, Athom and other plugs, relays, wall switches, lamps and LED strips with an ESP8266 or ESP32 chip.

Shelly devices with their own firmware: Gen2 and newer (Plus, Pro, Mini, Gen3, Gen4) with switching, bulbs, schedules and auto-off; Gen1 partly.

Devices with other firmware, such as eWeLink or Tuya, need Tasmota installed first.

Does my lamp go via a server in China?

No, your lamp stays home. When you tap a switch, the signal travels a few metres over your own Wi-Fi, straight from your phone to the switch. Many smart home apps first send it to a cloud server, sometimes on the other side of the world, and then back again, just to switch on the lamp right next to you. With this app, the trip to the lamp is shorter than the trip to the fridge.

Which phones can run it?

Android 7 or newer. Adding a brand-new device from the app needs Android 10 or newer. There is no iPhone version, but you can try the live demo in any browser.

Why is it not in the Play Store?

I make this app alone, in my spare time. Getting a new app into the Play Store means paying a fee, an identity check, and first finding 12 testers who keep it installed for 14 days. That is a lot of hassle for a free hobby app. So you download it straight from GitHub instead.

Android warns about installing it. Is it safe?

The app is not in the Play Store, so Google Play Protect has not seen it before. You can let Play Protect scan it first, or install without scanning. The full source code is public on GitHub, and GitHub itself builds the download from that source. Since 21 November 2026 I am a Google-registered developer, and the app is registered with Google’s Android developer verification. On most phones Android no longer asks to scan it.

Does it work with OpenBeken or Shelly?

OpenBeken devices partly work: switching does, schedules do not. Shelly devices work fully when they run Tasmota firmware.

New and untested: the app can now also find Shelly devices with their own firmware, switch them on and off, set brightness, white and colour on Shelly bulbs, rename them and set their auto-off, and add a new Shelly to your Wi-Fi (Gen1 to Gen4). Tested on a real Shelly 1 Gen3. Schedules work too on Gen2 and newer (same schedule cards, with sunrise and sunset), stored in the Shelly itself. Energy for Shelly is not there yet.

Why not use the Shelly app?

You don’t have to choose. Both apps can be on your phone and control the same Shelly.

The Shelly app is made by Shelly itself. It works with every Shelly, and only with Shelly. Also when you are away from home, and with Alexa or Google Home. You need a Shelly account for it.

This app is smaller and simpler. No account. Everything stays in your home. Your Tasmota and Shelly switches are on one screen.

They work side by side. Switch the lamp on in one app, and the other app shows it on too. Auto-off and schedules are stored in the Shelly itself, so they keep working, whichever app you used.

Note: this app makes schedules as on/off pairs: one on time and one off time per schedule. Schedules you make in the Shelly app may not show up in this app, but they keep working in the Shelly.

A good mix: the Shelly app to set up a new Shelly, this app for every day.

Why does it ask for my location?

For sunrise and sunset times, which the app sends to your switches. Android also needs location permission before an app can see nearby Wi-Fi names. Your location only goes to your own switches.

What is a VPN?

A VPN (virtual private network) is a private, encrypted connection between your own devices over the internet. With Tailscale VPN, your phone away from home connects to an old phone or tablet at home, as if you were on your home Wi-Fi. Only your own devices can use it, and you don't need to open any ports in your router. Tailscale is free for personal use.

Read the Tailscale VPN guide

How do I get updates?

Scroll to the bottom of the main screen and tap Check for update. The app downloads the newest version from GitHub and installs it.

How many switches can I add?

As many as you like. There is no limit on switches, groups, scenes or widgets, and no paid version that unlocks more. Only Tasmota itself has a limit: up to 8 on/off schedules per switch.

What does it cost?

Nothing. No ads, no in-app purchases, no subscription.

both started on the ESP8266,
a cheap Wi-Fi chip from Espressif.
Espressif was founded in Shanghai in 2008
by Teo Swee Ann from Singapore.
The ESP8266 came out in 2014,
and over 1.5 billion of their chips have been sold.

Fun fact:
The ESP8266 started as a 2-dollar Wi-Fi modem,
controlled with AT commands
from the 1980s telephone modem era.
At first, the documentation was hard to find,
and only in Chinese.
Hobbyists found out it could run its own programs,
and Tasmota and Shelly grew from that.

More: from modem to small computer
  • 2014: the ESP-01 board was first used as a cheap Wi-Fi add-on for an Arduino. The Arduino sent it AT commands, just like a modem.
  • Late 2014: Espressif released an official SDK, and NodeMCU appeared, which let you program the ESP8266 itself in the Lua language. This was the first big step from “modem” to “small computer”.
  • 2015: Ivan Grokhotkov made the ESP8266 Arduino core. From then on you could program the ESP8266 directly from the Arduino IDE, without an Arduino board at all. That’s what made it explode in popularity.
  • Tasmota is built on that ESP8266 Arduino core, so it grew out of the Arduino world.

Why free?

I made this app for my own home, for fun. I’m a handy guy with the lowest possible education, and I like home automation that just works. If it helps you too, that makes me happy.

Why no tracking?

The app talks to your own switches on your own Wi-Fi. No analytics: nothing about you or your home is sent anywhere. It only goes online to check GitHub for a new version, and for icon search if you use it.

Why no account?

There is no server to log in to. Your switches, your phone, your home.

Why no ads?

Ads bring tracking and clutter. The app stays small and quiet.
